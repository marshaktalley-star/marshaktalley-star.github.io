ACADEMIC SKILLS ASSESSMENT

Foundational Literacy and Numeracy Assessment

Identifying foundational reading, writing and mathematics skills to guide targeted instruction and intervention planning.

Assessment Instructions

This assessment examines foundational literacy and numeracy skills. Literacy and numeracy can be administered independently or together. Use the appropriate report button to review each area.

Students complete independent tasks while an educator or appropriately trained assessor administers oral activities and records responses.

This academic skills assessment is informed by established educational assessment methods.

Record relevant language background, attendance, previous instruction and accessibility needs. Language or dialect differences must not be treated as evidence of disability.

Fry and Dolch Word Inventories

Choose a list and record each word as Correct, Incorrect, or Not tested. Start at a manageable level. Present one word at a time without pictures or prompts. These are foundational word-frequency lists, not secondary-school grade-equivalent reading tests. Do not use sight-word scores alone to assign a reading level.

Use the word lists to find gaps, then teach letter–sound patterns, decoding, irregular spellings, meaning and sentence use. Assess independent reading before offering help. Word lists overlap; scores across lists are not additive.

Teaching activities for words not yet secure

Decodable words: teach the relevant grapheme–phoneme pattern, blend, build with letter cards, then read in a new sentence. Irregular words: identify regular sounds and the unexpected spelling, then practise retrieval and writing from dictation. Transfer: read a sentence and a short age-respectful paragraph containing target words; ask a literal and an inference question. Monitor: practise 5–10 selected words, check with an untaught mixed probe weekly, and record accurate independent responses.

The first 500 Fry words are provided here in five groups of 100. For Fry groups 501–1,000, consult the full Fry reference list. The Dolch inventory is complete (315 words). Source references: Dolch and Fry.

Structured Reading and Writing Inventory

Administer in sequence, starting at a level the student can attempt successfully. This is an informal instructional inventory, not a standardised grade-placement test. Record independent performance before giving support. Use a fresh, comparable probe to confirm progress.

For older learners, use age-respectful contexts even when teaching early decoding. Record whether the student read independently, needed prompts, or answered after listening. Jamaican Patois and Standard English differences must not be scored as a disability.

A. Word-reading ladder

Show each word separately without pictures or pronunciation cues. Tick words read correctly on the first independent attempt. Groups are instructional skill bands, not grade-equivalent scores. The high-frequency selections are original curated samples informed by common word-frequency teaching approaches; they are not the complete Fry or Dolch lists.

B. Sentence reading and understanding

Ask the student to read each sentence aloud, then answer the question without looking back where feasible. Record reading and comprehension separately. Stop when the text is too difficult and return to a more accessible band.

C. Paragraph reading and comprehension

Use unseen paragraphs. Record oral reading errors and comprehension independently. Questions include literal, vocabulary and inferential understanding; accept equivalent accurate responses.

D. Writing ladder

Offer paper or an accessible writing method. Do not score handwriting as spelling or composition. First collect an independent sample, then document the effect of modelling or prompts.

Finding a book's reading level

Use Lexile Find a Book for titles with Lexile measures, AR BookFinder for ATOS book levels, and publisher information where available. These systems are not interchangeable. A book level is a text-complexity indicator, not proof of a student's reading level. Confirm suitability using a short unseen passage, reading accuracy, comprehension, interest and age-appropriate content.

Literacy Assessment — Reading Comprehension

Student instructions: Read the passage independently and answer the questions that follow.

The School Garden

On Tuesday morning, Nia went to the school garden with her class. The students wanted to grow callaloo and tomatoes. Their teacher asked them to loosen the soil before planting. Nia noticed that one corner of the garden was dry, so she carried water from a nearby tank. By Friday, the class had planted twelve seedlings. They agreed to check the garden every morning because young plants need regular care.

This short original passage samples comprehension. It does not establish a grade or instructional reading level.

Numeracy Assessment

Complete the questions independently unless an access arrangement is documented. Questions progress from foundational to more complex examples within each domain. A few items cannot establish mastery; use error patterns to select follow-up probes.

Administration: Provide paper for working. Do not use a calculator unless the assessment accommodation is explicitly recorded. For mathematical reasoning, read the question aloud only when assessing mathematical reasoning rather than independent reading. Record any support given. No overall score should be interpreted as a standardised mathematics level.

Functional Learning and Access

Complete this observation profile alongside the academic tasks. It is not a diagnostic checklist. Record what the student actually does, in what conditions, and what changes when support is provided. Select Not observed where evidence is unavailable.

Student voice: What helps me learn?

Ask these questions conversationally. Record the student's words or their preferred method of communication. These responses are not scored.

Compare independent work and supported work

Where a difficulty is observed, try a brief, appropriate support such as modelling, chunking, visual instructions or extra processing time. Record whether performance improves. Do not change standard task conditions without documenting it.

Act promptly on immediate safeguarding and accessibility concerns through the school's established procedures. Do not wait for an academic score or a scheduled intervention review.

Functional Life Skills — Optional

Use this section when there is a specific concern about everyday independence, participation or applying skills outside academic tasks. It is appropriate for secondary students when tasks and expectations are age-appropriate. Do not assume reduced independence from a low literacy or numeracy score.

Use observation, a practical demonstration, student input and, where appropriate, caregiver input. Record the opportunity provided and actual assistance needed. These ratings are descriptive and are not adaptive-functioning standard scores.

Assessment Interpretation and Intervention Guidance

Interpreting Student Performance

This assessment provides information about a student's current academic performance, strengths and skills requiring further development.

A low score indicates difficulty with the specific skill or task assessed. It does not independently establish the underlying cause or confirm a disability.

Educators and assessment professionals should consider:

  • Performance across multiple learning domains.
  • Previous assessments and classroom work samples.
  • Attendance, instructional history and opportunities to learn.
  • Language background, communication and access needs.
  • Teacher observations and classroom performance.
  • Response to targeted instruction and accommodations.
  • Impact on participation, independence and progress.

Planning Appropriate Interventions

Assessment findings should inform targeted, measurable interventions based on demonstrated needs.

Instructional planning may include explicit teaching, scaffolded learning, differentiated instruction, appropriate accommodations and documented curriculum modifications where necessary.

Intervention plans should identify the priority skill, baseline, measurable goal, instructional strategies, responsible personnel and progress-monitoring procedures.

Further Assessment and Specialist Support

Significant or persistent difficulties may require consultation with learning support professionals or specialist assessment services.

Referrals should follow relevant school, district or national procedures. Appropriate educational support should continue while further assessment is being arranged.

Immediate safeguarding, accessibility or significant functional concerns should be addressed promptly, rather than waiting for a routine intervention review.

Assessment findings describe the skills demonstrated during the activities completed and should be considered alongside classroom evidence, learning history and professional judgement. Where further evaluation is appropriate, educators should use relevant validated assessments and established referral procedures.
