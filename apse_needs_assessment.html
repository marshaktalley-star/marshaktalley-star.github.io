Rural Jamaican school baseline: document what exists, what teachers already do, what students experience, and which practical systems need to be built.

1. School-Wide APSE Needs Assessment

An exploratory baseline, not a school inspection. Record informal practices and resource constraints alongside existing strengths. Use conversations, classroom visits and whatever records are available.

Developmental baseline: 0 Not in place · 1 Informal practice · 2 Partially established · 3 Established but inconsistent · 4 Consistently used · N/O Not observed or verified. A zero identifies a starting point, not failure. Record evidence and barriers separately. Not an official Ministry scale.

These templates are not official Ministry of Education forms. Confirm local APSE procedures, reporting expectations and student safeguarding requirements with your supervisor.

Recommended reporting structure

  1. Rural school context, travel/attendance considerations and assessment scope
  2. Strengths, informal practices and community assets
  3. Student learning needs and barriers
  4. Teacher development needs and material/infrastructure constraints (reported separately)
  5. Existing and missing systems: assessment, referral, intervention and progress review
  6. Three feasible priorities, including low-cost actions, responsible roles and review dates

Privacy: Share aggregated findings in general reports. Restrict access to raw survey responses and avoid small-group breakdowns that could identify individuals.
