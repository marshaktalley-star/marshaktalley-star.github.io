Scenario 1

The Extra Guests

Your reservation is for two people. During the stay, you discover that additional people have arrived at the property and at least one intends to stay overnight. They are not listed on the reservation.

Not the strongest choice.

Ignoring additional occupants can weaken your ability to enforce occupancy expectations later. If your listing or agreement limits guests, address the issue promptly and document the communication.

Best first step.

Communicate calmly, refer to the reservation and applicable house rules, and ask the guest to correct the issue. Keep important reservation-related communication on the platform whenever appropriate.

Pause before escalating.

Immediate cancellation may not be necessary or permitted in every circumstance. Establish what happened, document it, review your rules and platform procedures, and use the appropriate escalation process if the guest does not resolve the issue.

Time is up.

The key question is not simply whether the guest annoyed you. Ask: What does my listing say, what did the guest agree to, and what process should I follow before acting?

Scenario 2

The Cleaner Says It's Done

Your cleaner messages you: “Finished 👍.” Your next guest arrives and sends photographs showing that the bathroom was not properly cleaned.

Resolve proportionately.

Compensation may sometimes be appropriate, but first understand the problem and determine what can be corrected. A full refund should not automatically be the first response to every service failure.

The guest booked with you.

Your cleaner may have caused the problem, but from the guest's perspective the property is your responsibility. Resolve the guest issue first. Address service-provider performance separately.

Best response.

Acknowledge the concern, document the condition, arrange a reasonable solution, and then use your service-provider agreement or documented expectations to address the cleaner's performance. Systems are not only for guests.

Time is up.

Your service providers affect your reviews, your reputation and your revenue. Clear written expectations and completion verification help protect both the guest experience and your property.

Scenario 3

The Long-Stay Extension

A guest books your property for 30 days. Halfway through the stay, they tell you they need another two weeks but cannot pay until later. They have already received mail at the property.

High-risk choice.

Allowing someone to remain without payment or without clarifying the legal and booking arrangement may create financial and occupancy problems. Do not make an informal extension simply because the guest promises to pay later.

This may come next — but not first.

If extending the stay is lawful and appropriate, keeping the reservation and payment on Airbnb may be the cleaner approach. But first understand whether the additional stay creates different legal obligations where your property is located.

Correct first response: C.

Check the local laws, community rules and occupancy implications before agreeing to the extension. If the extension is appropriate, then the next step may be B: extend the reservation through Airbnb and keep the booking, payment and communication on the platform. A private rental arrangement is a different relationship and should be handled accordingly.

Do not assume immediate removal is the answer.

The guest's legal occupancy status can depend on the jurisdiction and circumstances. Understand the law and the applicable process before deciding what action you can take.

Time is up.

Platform rules do not replace local law. Long stays deserve additional attention because the legal relationship between host and occupant may change depending on location and circumstances.

💡
The Prepared Host Principle

The system helps you know what to check before you act.

Hosting is not only about being welcoming. Clear expectations, documentation, local-law awareness, reliable service providers and consistent processes help you respond thoughtfully when something goes wrong.

Don't just be a good host. Be a prepared host.
