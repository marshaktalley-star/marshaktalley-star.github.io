BUILD
THE SPACE

Question: If you had the power to redesign this room, what would you change—and what would it cost?

This lesson treats adolescents as adolescents. Reading and mathematics are embedded in a real design challenge, with multiple ways to participate, communicate, move, calculate and create.

J$50,000

Your team has a fixed budget to improve the learning space.

  • Measure the room
  • Choose what matters
  • Build a realistic budget
  • Create the design
  • Pitch the decision

Duration: 60–75 minutes

Classroom Ecology in Practice

The environment is part of the intervention, not background scenery.

MovementStudents measure, rotate roles, inspect and build rather than remain seated for the whole lesson.
LightUse softer lamps or indirect lighting where facility rules allow; avoid unnecessary sensory harshness.
NaturePlants can soften an institutional environment and give learners responsibility for a shared space.
VoiceLearners are asked what they would change. Their observations become legitimate lesson content.
StructureClear stages, visible expectations and predictable transitions reduce uncertainty without lowering expectations.
Interview rationale: Discipline is only one part of classroom behaviour. The design of the environment, opportunities for movement, sensory load, learner voice and predictability can influence regulation and engagement. The aim is not to make the space decorative; it is to make learning more accessible and the room more human.

2. Learning Target

I can use reading, speaking, measurement and money skills to design a better learning space and explain the choices I make.

Learning Outcomes

By the end of the lesson, I can:
  • contribute an idea to redesign a real space;
  • measure or estimate dimensions and quantities;
  • work within a J$50,000 budget;
  • read or interpret prices, numbers, symbols and functional words.
I can also:
  • communicate and defend a decision;
  • work independently or collaboratively;
  • use literacy and numeracy in a meaningful task;
  • identify how the physical environment can affect learning and behaviour.

3. Assignment Sequence

The original Weekly Assignment structure is retained as a reusable Monday–Friday extension, while today's core lesson can be completed in one 60–75 minute session.

11. Compare & Contrast

Use two sources: for example, a photo of a traditional institutional classroom and a photo/article about a more flexible learning environment.

Use images and short, readable text so nonreaders can participate through observation and discussion. Do not use infantile clip art. A learner can compare visual evidence before being asked to decode a paragraph.

15. Closing Discussion

Does your environment affect how you behave?

What makes a space feel safe without somebody having to tell you it is safe?

The discussion is about the environment—not personal trauma. Learners are never required to disclose abuse, abandonment, family relationships or the reasons they are in care.

16. Assessment

Choose an answer and explain why. The explanation is more important than guessing correctly.

1. Your budget is J$50,000. You spend J$32,000. What is your balance?
2. Which word best means the amount of money you still have left?
3. A small plant costs J$2,000. What do four small plants cost?
4. Why do we measure the room before planning the layout?
5. Which is the best example of a design decision?
6. What does exceeding the budget mean?
7. A rug costs J$12,000 and a floor lamp costs J$8,500. What is the total?
8. Which sentence best supports an opinion with evidence?
9. Your budget is cut from J$50,000 to J$40,000. How much was removed?
10. Which statement best explains the lesson?

17. Special Education Access & Differentiation

Reading

Use photographs, prices, symbols, oral directions, repeated functional words and modelling. Keep the content adolescent.

Writing

Allow dictation, sketching, selecting, typing, recording numbers or oral presentation.

Numeracy

Use real money contexts, calculators, measuring tapes and concrete quantities.

Attention / regulation

Give an active role and allow purposeful movement rather than treating movement as automatic noncompliance.

Group access

Offer an individual design route when group interaction is too demanding.

Re-entry

Allow a learner to step away briefly and return without humiliation or a public power struggle.

18. Classroom Environment

Use plants, warmer/indirect lighting, organised zones and age-appropriate displays where facility rules allow. Essential oils should only be considered with facility approval and after checking asthma, allergies, medication and sensory sensitivities. Fragrance should never be necessary for participation.

The objective is not to make the classroom “pretty.” The objective is to create conditions that make learning, regulation, dignity and participation more possible.
