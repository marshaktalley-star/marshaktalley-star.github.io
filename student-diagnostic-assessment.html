APSE Student Academic Assessment & Intervention Baseline

Jamaican Secondary Education | School-Developed Tool

Assessment purpose

This assessment identifies academic skills requiring additional instruction and helps establish an individual student baseline.

Important: This is an original, informal educational assessment. It is not a standardised diagnostic instrument, a medical assessment, an IQ test or a determination of special education eligibility.

Use findings alongside school records, classroom observations, previous assessments, family input and response to instruction.

Do not delay appropriate learning support while awaiting a specialist assessment.

1. Student information

2. Foundational reading

Administration: Work individually with the student. Show words one at a time. Do not provide feedback during scoring.

Real words:
sun, market, plant, basket, window, tomorrow, careful, information
Invented words:
mip, shab, fenter, plim, noster, vake

Explain that these are made-up words. Accept a consistent plausible pronunciation when English spelling allows more than one. Record the actual response for ambiguous items.

These short word lists are qualitative screening probes. They have not been validated for Jamaican students. Use a recognised reading inventory when available.

3. Reading comprehension and fluency

Student passage: Read the passage independently.

On Tuesday morning, Nia went to the school garden with her class. The students wanted to grow callaloo and tomatoes. Their teacher asked them to loosen the soil before planting. Nia noticed that one corner of the garden was dry, so she carried water from a nearby tank. By Friday, the class had planted twelve seedlings. They agreed to check the garden every morning because young plants need regular care.

Optional oral-reading probe

Ask the student to read the same passage aloud. Record errors and time. Because the student may already have read the passage, treat this as a descriptive sample rather than an independent standardised fluency measure.

The passage contains 70 words using whitespace-based word counting. Correct words per minute is calculated from words attempted minus errors, divided by time in minutes. It is not compared with US or UK norms.

4. Mathematics assessment

Give the student paper for working. For mathematics-only assessment, directions may be explained orally without giving the solution. Record whether oral support was provided.

5. Written expression

Student task:

Write four to six sentences about something you learned, made or helped with. Explain what happened and why it mattered.

Allow up to ten minutes. If the student cannot write independently, record what happens rather than supplying the words.

Score each feature from 0 to 2. These ratings are descriptive, not standardised.

Jamaican Patois must not be treated as evidence of a disability. Record Standard Jamaican English writing conventions separately from the student's ability to communicate ideas.

6. Listening comprehension

The assessor reads the following passage aloud. Do not show the student the text.

Mr Brown brought three boxes of books to the library. Each box had five books. He placed ten books on the shelves and left the rest on the table. The librarian thanked him because the new books would help students practise reading.

Read once at a natural pace. Repeat once if needed and record that repetition. Ask the questions orally.

7. Functional learning and access

Record observations without assigning a diagnosis.

Immediate safeguarding, sensory-access or significant functional concerns must be addressed through the appropriate school procedures. Do not wait for assessment scores or an intervention review.

8. Results and intervention planning

Assessment interpretation guidance

A low score demonstrates difficulty with the specific task administered. It does not establish the cause of the difficulty.

The APSE Coach should consider:

  • Performance across several learning domains
  • Previous school assessment results
  • Opportunity to learn and attendance
  • Student language and access needs
  • Classroom work and teacher observations
  • Response to explicit, targeted instruction
  • Functional impact on classroom participation

Consider consultation with the school's student support personnel and the Regional Special Needs Coordinator when difficulties are severe, persistent or extend beyond academic skills.

Specialist assessment may be appropriate before a six-week intervention period has elapsed. Academic intervention and referral can proceed at the same time.

School-developed tool. Not an official MOE assessment, IDRI replacement, psychoeducational evaluation or validated special education eligibility instrument.
