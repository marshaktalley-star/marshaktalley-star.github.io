The Concept

What if we gave connection more than a few minutes?

Traditional speed dating asks us to make quick decisions: attraction or no attraction, yes or no, interested or not.

But meaningful connection does not always reveal itself in five minutes.

Six Weeks to Date Differently brings a group of singles together over six facilitated sessions. Participants meet, talk, reflect, rotate, reconnect and gradually explore compatibility while also examining the beliefs and patterns they may be bringing into relationships.

This isn't about forcing a match. It is about creating enough time and structure for people to notice more than first impressions.

“You don't have to know on the first night whether someone could be your future. Sometimes you simply need enough space to discover who they are.”

The Experience

Dating meets personal development.

Each week combines guided reflection, Love Affirmations, intentional conversations and dating rotations so participants can practise healthier ways of connecting while actually meeting potential partners.

1

Meet

Meet a curated group of singles in a relaxed, structured environment without the pressure of deciding everything immediately.

2

Notice

Pay attention to attraction, assumptions, fear, comparison, communication and the patterns that appear while dating.

3

Practise

Practise communicating clearly, receiving interest, setting boundaries and staying curious rather than automatically repeating familiar patterns.

The Six-Week Journey

Six weeks. Six conversations with yourself—and others.

Each session has its own relationship theme and Love Affirmation. The dating conversations become progressively deeper as participants get to know one another.

Week 1

What Am I Bringing Into Love?

“I can meet someone new without asking them to repair something old.”

Week One is about introductions, curiosity and first impressions. Participants begin exploring what they want from a relationship and what previous experiences may have taught them to expect from love.

Reflection What have my previous relationships taught me to expect?
Dating Experience Short, relaxed rotations designed primarily for introductions, humour and natural conversation.
Takeaway Notice who surprised you. No pressure to eliminate anyone based solely on first impressions.
Week 2

I Do Not Have to Compete for Love

“I do not have to compete for love.”

Participants explore comparison, jealousy, pursuit, rejection and what it means to feel intentionally chosen. The dating environment itself provides opportunities to notice these feelings without immediately reacting to them.

Reflection What happens inside me when someone I like is also connecting with other people?
Dating Experience New rotations with prompts around attraction, intentionality and what being chosen means.
Takeaway Interest does not require competition, performance or abandoning yourself.
Week 3

What Does Safe Love Feel Like?

“I deserve love that feels real, honest, safe, mutual and clear.”

This session explores emotional safety, consistency, boundaries, respect and clarity. Participants consider the difference between intense chemistry and genuine relational safety.

Reflection What behaviours actually make me feel safe, respected and considered?
Dating Experience Longer conversations around trust, communication, affection and relationship expectations.
Takeaway Begin noticing not only who excites you, but how you feel around them.
Week 4

Receiving Love Without Expecting Pain

“I can receive something good without searching for the moment it will disappear.”

Participants explore what can happen when genuine attention, affection or consistency feels unfamiliar. The focus is on recognising protective patterns without shaming ourselves for having developed them.

Reflection Do I become suspicious, distant or uninterested when someone treats me well?
Dating Experience Intentional conversations about vulnerability, trust and how each person responds to closeness.
Takeaway Protection and avoidance are not always the same thing.
Week 5

I Can Say What I Need

“My needs do not make me difficult to love.”

By Week Five, conversations move beyond attraction into compatibility. Participants explore whether their actual lives, expectations and values could work together.

Reflection What do I genuinely need from a partner—and what have I been afraid to say?
Dating Experience Longer conversations covering values, children, lifestyle, money, communication, affection, future plans and relationship goals.
Takeaway Chemistry matters. Compatibility matters too.
Week 6

Choosing Without Abandoning Myself

“I can choose love without losing myself.”

The final session brings together everything participants have noticed during the programme. There is space for longer conversations, reflection, mutual matches and celebration.

Reflection What have I learned about how I choose, receive and respond to connection?
Dating Experience Intentional longer dates with people participants would genuinely like to continue getting to know.
Takeaway Leave with greater clarity about how you want to date—whether or not you leave with a match.
How Each Evening Works

Structured enough to feel intentional.
Relaxed enough to feel like dating.

This is not six weeks of sitting in a circle talking about relationships. The personal-development component supports the dating experience rather than replacing it.

01. Arrive Informal social time, refreshments and an opportunity to settle into the room.
02. Reflect A short facilitated conversation introduces the week's relationship theme and affirmation.
03. Date Guided dating rotations use carefully selected prompts while leaving room for natural conversation and chemistry.
04. Notice Private reflection helps participants notice attraction, reactions, assumptions and potential connections.
Why This Is Different

More than speed dating.

No five-minute verdicts.

Participants have multiple opportunities to interact before deciding whether they would like to explore a connection further.

No weekly popularity contest.

Preferences remain private. Participants are not publicly told who selected or rejected them. Mutual interest is handled thoughtfully.

Real relationship conversations.

Questions gradually move beyond careers and hobbies toward values, communication, boundaries, family, lifestyle and future expectations.

Success isn't defined by leaving coupled.

Participants can leave with stronger self-awareness, healthier dating practices and meaningful connections even when a romantic match does not develop.

Who It's For

Singles who want to date with greater intention.

Participants don't need to have everything figured out. They simply need to be genuinely available to meet people and willing to approach dating with curiosity and respect.

  • Single and genuinely available to date
  • Interested in a meaningful relationship
  • Open to meeting people beyond immediate first impressions
  • Willing to communicate respectfully
  • Interested in understanding personal relationship patterns
  • Able to commit to the six-week experience
  • Respectful of other participants' privacy and boundaries
  • Comfortable accepting that attraction and matches cannot be guaranteed
Matching & Privacy

Connection without unnecessary pressure.

Interest stays private.

Participants can privately indicate who they would like additional time with as the programme progresses. Individual selections are not publicly revealed.

Where mutual interest exists, participants may be given opportunities for longer conversations during later sessions. Contact information should only be exchanged with consent.

Participants are expected to respect confidentiality, personal boundaries and each person's right to change their mind at any point.

The programme creates opportunities for connection. It does not guarantee romantic compatibility, a match, a relationship or any particular outcome.

Questions

Before you join.

Is this therapy?

No. Six Weeks to Date Differently is a facilitated personal-development and social dating experience. It is not psychotherapy, counselling or mental-health treatment.

Am I guaranteed to meet a partner?

No. The programme creates structured opportunities to meet and get to know other singles, but attraction, compatibility and relationships cannot be guaranteed.

What happens if I like someone early in the programme?

That's perfectly fine. Participants are encouraged to remain curious while the programme progresses. Mutual interest may lead to additional conversation time without turning the programme into a public competition.

What if nobody chooses me?

Romantic matching is only one part of the experience. The programme is also designed to help participants better understand how they date, communicate, choose and respond to connection. Individual preferences will not be publicly announced.

Can I miss a week?

Because the same cohort progresses together, consistent attendance is important. Participants should plan to attend all six sessions wherever possible.

Who can participate?

Eligibility, age range, dating preferences and cohort structure will be confirmed for each programme before registration opens.
