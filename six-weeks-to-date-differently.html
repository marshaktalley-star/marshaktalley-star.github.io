The Idea

What if connection had more than a few minutes?

Traditional speed dating asks us to decide quickly: attraction or no attraction, yes or no, interested or not.

But meaningful connection does not always reveal itself in five minutes.

Six Weeks to Date Differently brings a group of singles together over six facilitated sessions. Participants meet, talk, rotate, reconnect and gradually explore compatibility while also noticing the beliefs and patterns they may be bringing into relationships.

The goal is not to force a match. It is to create enough time and structure for people to notice more than first impressions.

“You don't have to know on the first night whether someone could be your future. Sometimes you simply need enough space to discover who they are.”

Relationships & Self-Awareness

Understanding Your Attachment Style

How do you respond when love feels uncertain?

I hope you had a chance to listen to my voice note. Attachment styles are often shaped by early childhood experiences, but our experiences throughout life can also influence how we connect with others.

Remember, these are simply tools to help you understand yourself better. They are not the be-all and end-all. Attachment patterns are not just about ego or insecurity, and they are not personality flaws.

Take a moment to review the graphic. Can you identify which attachment style best describes you?

What happens when connection feels uncertain?

Do you become anxious when someone takes longer than usual to respond? Do you withdraw when someone gets too close? Or can you comfortably give and receive affection without constantly questioning the relationship?

Our attachment patterns can influence how we experience intimacy, communicate our needs, respond to conflict and interpret another person's behaviour.

Understanding these patterns isn't about putting yourself or someone else into a box. It's about recognising behaviours that may be helping or hurting your relationships.

Four Common Attachment Styles

These are common patterns, not fixed identities or diagnoses. You may recognise elements of more than one.

Secure Attachment

You are generally comfortable with intimacy and independence. You can communicate your needs, respect boundaries and navigate uncertainty without automatically assuming the relationship is threatened.

Ways to strengthen secure connection

Communicate openly, keep healthy boundaries, repair misunderstandings and remain curious about your partner's needs.

When your partner is securely attached

Be direct about your needs, appreciate consistency and allow room for both closeness and independence.

Anxious Attachment

You may desire closeness and reassurance but become unsettled when communication changes or affection feels inconsistent. Learning to regulate your emotions and communicate your needs can help.

Ways to become more secure

Pause before reacting, name what you feel, practise self-soothing and ask clearly for reassurance rather than assuming the worst.

When your partner has anxious patterns

Be consistent where you can, communicate changes in availability, listen without dismissing their feelings and maintain honest boundaries.

Avoidant Attachment

You may value independence and find emotional vulnerability uncomfortable. Learning to communicate your need for space while remaining emotionally available can support healthier relationships.

Ways to become more secure

Notice the urge to withdraw, explain when you need space, agree on when to reconnect and practise sharing feelings in manageable steps.

When your partner has avoidant patterns

Respect reasonable space without abandoning your own needs. Ask for clarity and agree on ways to stay connected without pressure.

Fearful-Avoidant Attachment

You may desire connection while simultaneously fearing it. You might move towards someone and then withdraw when the relationship becomes emotionally close. Developing emotional awareness, trust and consistency can help.

Ways to become more secure

Notice what triggers the push-and-pull response, practise grounding before reacting and build trust through small, consistent steps. Seek additional support when useful.

When your partner has fearful-avoidant patterns

Offer steady, respectful communication, avoid chasing or punishing withdrawal, and discuss boundaries and repair when both of you are calm.

Your Attachment Style Is Not Your Destiny

Everyone deserves to be loved, respected and treated well, regardless of their attachment style.

Your attachment patterns are not permanent labels. With awareness, practice and supportive relationships, you can develop more secure ways of connecting.

The goal isn't to find someone who never triggers your insecurities. It's to recognise your patterns, communicate your needs and learn how to respond rather than simply react.

What Does This Have to Do With Dating Differently?

Everything.

Six Weeks to Date Differently creates opportunities to meet people while reflecting on how you approach attraction, uncertainty, communication, emotional safety and compatibility.

You don't have to arrive with everything figured out. You simply have to be willing to notice your patterns and practise something different.

Practical Tools

Self-Soothing Exercises

For moments when uncertainty in a relationship brings up anxiety. Self-soothing can help you respond with more clarity instead of reacting from fear.

Try one small step at a time.

Breathe slowly
Name what you feel
Use your senses in the present moment
Use a soothing mantra
Move your body
Engage your senses
Journal it out
Distract with intention
Seek support when you need it

Self-soothing doesn't mean pretending everything is fine or ignoring genuine concerns. It means giving yourself space to settle your emotions so you can decide what to communicate, what to accept and what needs to change.

Remember: You can work on your emotional responses and still expect mutual effort, respect and consistency in your relationships.

The Six-Week Journey

Six weeks. Six opportunities to date differently.

The conversations become progressively deeper as participants get to know one another. Each week's Love Affirmation gives the group something specific to explore and practise.

Choose a week to open its outline.

Week1 What Am I Bringing Into Love?
“I can meet someone new without asking them to repair something old.”

The first evening is about curiosity rather than conclusions. Participants meet the group, explore what they are looking for and begin noticing what previous experiences may have taught them to expect from love.

ReflectWhat have previous relationships taught me to expect?
DateShort, relaxed rotations focused on introductions, humour and natural conversation.
NoticeWho surprised you? What did you assume before you actually spoke to someone?
Week2 I Do Not Have to Compete for Love
“I do not have to compete for love.”

This week explores comparison, jealousy, pursuit and what happens internally when someone we like is also getting to know other people.

ReflectWhat happens inside me when someone I like has other options?
DateRotations explore attraction, intentionality and what being chosen means to each person.
NoticeInterest does not require competition, performance or abandoning yourself.
Week3 What Does Safe Love Feel Like?
“I deserve love that feels real, honest, safe, mutual and clear.”

Participants explore emotional safety, consistency, respect, boundaries and clarity—and the difference between feeling intensely attracted to someone and feeling genuinely safe with them.

ReflectWhat behaviours actually make me feel safe and considered?
DateLonger conversations around trust, communication, affection and expectations.
NoticePay attention not only to who excites you, but how you feel around them.
Week4 Receiving Love Without Expecting Pain
“I can receive something good without searching for the moment it will disappear.”

Sometimes healthy attention feels unfamiliar. This session explores what can happen when affection, consistency or genuine interest activates old expectations of disappointment.

ReflectDo I become suspicious, distant or uninterested when someone treats me well?
DateConversations explore vulnerability, trust and how each person responds to closeness.
NoticeProtecting yourself and avoiding connection are not always the same thing.
Week5 I Can Say What I Need
“My needs do not make me difficult to love.”

By Week Five, conversations move beyond attraction and toward compatibility. Participants explore whether their values, expectations and actual lives could work together.

ReflectWhat do I genuinely need from a partner—and what have I been afraid to say?
DateConversations may include children, lifestyle, money, communication, affection and future goals.
NoticeChemistry matters. Compatibility matters too.
Week6 Choosing Without Abandoning Myself
“I can choose love without losing myself.”

The final evening brings the experience together. Participants reflect on what they have learned about themselves, spend more intentional time with people they would like to know further and celebrate the completion of the six weeks.

ReflectWhat have I learned about how I choose, receive and respond to connection?
DateLonger intentional conversations with potential mutual connections.
Leave WithGreater clarity about how you want to date, whether or not you leave with a match.

Learning to receive something different.

Love Affirmations are woven throughout the six-week experience as prompts for reflection—not promises, magic words or a way of convincing someone to choose us.

They are reminders that we can question old beliefs, notice automatic responses and practise healthier ways of approaching connection.

Why This Is Different

More than speed dating.

No five-minute verdicts. Participants have multiple opportunities to interact before deciding whether they would like to explore a connection further.
No weekly popularity contest. Preferences remain private. Participants are not publicly told who selected or rejected them.
Real conversations. Questions gradually move beyond careers and hobbies toward values, communication, boundaries, family, lifestyle and expectations.
A match isn't the only measure of success. Participants can leave with greater self-awareness, healthier dating practices and meaningful connections even if romance does not develop.
Who It's For

Singles who want to date with greater intention.

You don't need to have love figured out. You simply need to be genuinely available to meet people and willing to approach the experience with curiosity and respect.

  • Single and genuinely available to date
  • Interested in meaningful connection
  • Open to looking beyond immediate first impressions
  • Willing to communicate respectfully
  • Curious about personal relationship patterns
  • Able to commit to the six-week experience
  • Respectful of privacy and boundaries
  • Understands that attraction and matches cannot be guaranteed
Connection & Privacy

No public choosing. No public rejection.

Interest stays private.

As the programme progresses, participants may privately indicate who they would like additional time with. Individual selections are not publicly revealed.

Where mutual interest exists, participants may be given opportunities for longer conversations during later sessions. Contact information is only exchanged with consent.

Participants are expected to respect confidentiality, personal boundaries and each person's right to change their mind.

The programme creates opportunities for connection. It does not guarantee romantic compatibility, a match or a relationship.

A Few Questions

Before you join.

Is this therapy?

No. Six Weeks to Date Differently is a facilitated personal-development and social dating experience. It is not psychotherapy, counselling or mental-health treatment.

Am I guaranteed to meet a partner?

No. The programme creates structured opportunities to meet and get to know other singles, but attraction, compatibility and relationships cannot be guaranteed.

What if I like someone early?

That's okay. Participants are encouraged to remain curious while the programme progresses. Mutual interest may lead to additional conversation time without turning the experience into a public competition.

What if I don't make a romantic match?

A romantic match is not the only outcome. The programme is also an opportunity to understand more about how you date, communicate, choose and respond to connection.

Can I miss a week?

Because the same cohort progresses together, consistent attendance is important. Participants should plan to attend all six sessions wherever possible.

Who can participate?

Eligibility, age range, dating preferences and cohort structure will be confirmed before registration opens.
