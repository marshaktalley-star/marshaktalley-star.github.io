01 Before you book the shipment

Get your Jamaican identity and returning-resident documents in motion.

The easiest paperwork is the paperwork you do before your belongings are sitting at the port.

🇯🇲

Confirm Returning Resident eligibility

Jamaica Customs currently says a Jamaican national age 18+ who has lived overseas for the previous three consecutive years and is returning to live permanently may qualify, along with certain spouses, students and deportees.

Approval is not automatic. Expect to provide evidence and be interviewed.
Official checkpoint
🪪

Apply for your TRN early

Your Taxpayer Registration Number is one of the most useful identifiers to have in place. It appears in Returning Resident, vehicle, tax and other Jamaican processes.

Keep both a digital copy and a paper copy of the number with your move documents.
📂

Create one “move file”

Keep passport copies, proof of overseas residence, tax records, employment/separation records, proof of Jamaican address, vehicle documents, invoices, permits and shipping correspondence together.

Save it in the cloud and offline. Your phone may not always have data when you need a document.
🏦

Prepare local banking

If possible, open or reactivate a Jamaican account before the move and build a JMD balance for local transactions, deposits and everyday expenses.

Ask the bank in advance which proof-of-address, reference, tax-residency and source-of-funds documents it requires.
🧾

Make a detailed household inventory

List what you are shipping, quantities and reasonable values. Separate used household effects from new items and keep receipts for recently purchased goods when available.

Photograph valuable items and the condition of your shipment before it is sealed.
💰

Build a landing fund

Budget beyond shipping. You may face clearance, broker, port/storage, transport, housing deposits, utilities, insurance, food and temporary-car costs close together.

Keep a contingency reserve rather than planning your move down to the last dollar.
02 Shipping belongings

Know the full chain before your possessions leave the UK.

1

Ask whether your car and personal effects can travel in the same container

In my experience, consolidating them can reduce duplicated shipping-related charges and paperwork. Get a written comparison of the total cost before choosing separate shipments.

2

Choose the broker as carefully as the shipping company

Your experience on the Jamaican side matters. Ask for the name, licence/credentials and contact information of the person or company that will handle clearance before you pay and before the cargo departs.

3

Demand a written cost breakdown

Ask what is included, what is excluded, who pays port and handling charges, when storage begins, what the broker fee covers, and what happens if the shipment is delayed.

4

Confirm the bill of lading and estimated arrival

Check the spelling of your name, consignee information, vehicle details and destination. Keep the final bill of lading accessible.

5

Do not ship restricted items casually

Check whether anything in your container needs a permit. Jamaica Customs specifically notes that pets require a Veterinary Services import permit and plants require Plant Quarantine approval before importation.

Official checkpoint
03 Importing a vehicle

Do the permit work before the vehicle is shipped.

This is one area where assumptions become expensive very quickly.

🚗

Get the Trade Board import permit first

Current official guidance requires the motor-vehicle import permit before shipping arrangements are made. Returning Residents apply through JSWIFT and must submit supporting records.

Official requirement
🗓️

Correct ownership period: at least 6 months

For the Returning Resident motor-vehicle category, current Trade Board and Customs guidance says the vehicle must have been owned for at least six months before the application. Keep the title/registration and evidence showing when you purchased it.

Important: Returning Resident status does not make a vehicle duty-free.
Official clarification
🔍

Arrange pre-shipment inspection

Used vehicles shipped from the UK are subject to pre-shipment inspection/sanitisation requirements. Do not leave this until the vehicle is already on the water.

Official requirement
📑

Prepare the vehicle document pack

Expect to need the import permit, title/export certificate, bill of lading, supplier invoice, TCC and the Customs declaration prepared through your licensed Customs Broker.

Ask the dealership/seller for a properly completed invoice or bill of sale when you buy — not months later when you are trying to ship.
✅

Get your TCC

Jamaica Customs says a Tax Compliance Certificate is required for motor-vehicle clearance. Handle this early enough that a tax-compliance issue does not hold up the vehicle.

Official requirement
🤝

A licensed Customs Broker clears the vehicle

Jamaica Customs states that motor vehicles must be cleared by a licensed Customs Broker. Know exactly who that is before the vehicle arrives.

Official requirement
Do not rely on an informal arrangement where the vehicle is imported “in somebody else’s name” simply to avoid paperwork. The importer named in the documents carries legal and financial significance. If a broker proposes a structure you do not understand, get the explanation in writing before proceeding.
04 When you arrive

Be ready to move when Customs and your agent are ready.

📲

Stay reachable

Arrival at the port does not mean immediate release. In my experience, allowing roughly one to two weeks for clearance is sensible, but the actual time can vary.

Once you receive the release/collection message, act quickly so avoidable storage charges do not accumulate.
📞

Keep a simple contact sheet

Save your shipping line, local agent, broker, port/warehouse, insurer and vehicle transport contacts in one place.

🔐

Inspect before you leave

When practical, photograph your shipment or vehicle at collection and note visible damage immediately. Keep receipts and written records of anything disputed.

05 Money & transactions

Use Jamaican dollars intentionally and protect your payment options.

Use JMD for Jamaican expenses where practical

I learned this one the hard way. Paying in foreign currency or repeatedly converting through cards, merchants and banks can quietly cost you through exchange-rate spreads and fees.

Before a large purchase, compare the JMD price with the card issuer’s rate and foreign-transaction fee.

Use traceable payments for large purchases

For major purchases, favour payment methods that give you records and meaningful consumer protection. Credit cards can provide dispute options depending on your bank and the transaction.

Do not wire, Zelle or send irreversible money to strangers simply because you are being pressured to act quickly.

Keep some cash — not all your cash

Cash is still useful for markets, small vendors and local transactions, but avoid carrying more than you need. Use ATMs and banks thoughtfully and keep emergency cash separate.

Price the whole transaction

Ask for the final JMD amount including GCT, delivery, installation, broker fees or other charges. A quoted base price is not always the amount you will actually pay.

06 Healthcare & social protection

Know what is public, what is private, and what you need to register for.

🏥

Public healthcare: the no-user-fee policy

Jamaica’s Ministry of Health states that the no-user-fee policy remains in effect for public patients who are Jamaican residents and are not being treated as private or insured patients. Non-residents and certain private/insured categories can be charged.

“Free healthcare” does not mean every possible medicine, test, private consultation or service is guaranteed at no cost. Many returnees keep private insurance or an emergency medical fund as a supplement.
Official policy
🧓🏽

Apply for NIS if it applies to you

The National Insurance Scheme is Jamaica’s contributory social-security programme. The Ministry says people ages 18–70 who are gainfully occupied in insurable employment — including self-employed people — are required to register.

Registration uses the prescribed NIS application plus proof of age/identity through the relevant parish office.
Official programme
07 Driving & getting around

Sort out your driving documents and temporary transport early.

If you need a Jamaican provisional licence

Current transport guidance lists a completed application, three certified passport photographs, proof of residence/identity, a medical certificate, TRN and proof of age for the learner/provisional process.

The medical certificate used for the provisional application should not be more than three months old when submitted.
Official guidance

Do not assume one licence rule fits every situation

Whether you need to obtain or convert to a Jamaican licence immediately can depend on your status, the licence you already hold, insurance requirements and the transaction you are completing. Confirm the current requirement with the Island Traffic Authority/Tax Administration rather than relying on hearsay.

Renting can be cheaper than repeated private rides

If you live where route taxis are limited, a short-term rental can provide more freedom and may cost less than repeated private taxis or ride-hailing trips while you wait for your own vehicle.

Get transport prices before you move

Ask residents what normal fares are and compare options before committing. New arrivals can easily overpay when they do not yet know typical local prices.

08 Power, internet & island resilience

Use the sun, plan for outages and do not leave connectivity to chance.

Electricity and reliable internet affect almost everything when you are settling in — especially if you work remotely. I learned that having a modem in the house or being told a service is available does not necessarily mean you will have dependable service on move-in day.

☀️

Take advantage of Jamaica’s solar potential

If you own your property, price a properly designed rooftop solar system with battery storage from several reputable installers. Compare the total system — panels, inverter, batteries, installation, warranty, after-sales support and expected output — rather than comparing panel prices alone.

JPS currently offers a Net Billing programme for approved customers with renewable-energy systems who want to sell qualifying excess generation back to the grid. Ask your installer what approvals and equipment are required before assuming your system can export electricity.
Official programme available
🔋

Renting? Portable solar is still useful

You do not have to install permanent rooftop panels to benefit from the sun. Portable solar panels paired with a portable power station can keep phones, laptops, lights, Wi-Fi equipment and other small devices running during an outage.

Before buying, calculate what you actually need to power and for how many hours. A small system that can comfortably run your router and work laptop may be more useful than an oversized system you cannot easily move.
⚡

Do not underestimate air-conditioning costs

Electricity use can add up quickly. Running air-conditioning throughout the day the way you may have done overseas can produce a surprisingly high bill. Use AC strategically rather than automatically.

Blackout or thermal curtains help reduce solar heat gain. Keep curtains closed during the hottest part of the day, use fans where comfortable, and open windows when the outside air is actually cooler — early morning, evenings and after rain can be much better than opening the house during peak heat.
🌀

Have backup power for hurricane season

If your housing arrangement allows it, consider a properly sized generator or battery backup before storm season, not after an outage begins. Keep fuel and generator use within manufacturer and fire-safety guidance.

Never operate a fuel-burning generator inside a house, garage or other enclosed area. Carbon monoxide can kill. Keep battery banks charged when severe weather is approaching.
💧

Harvest rainwater carefully

Rainwater can be valuable for watering plants, washing outdoor areas and other non-potable uses. If you own the property, a properly designed catchment and covered tank can provide useful water resilience during interruptions or dry periods.

Keep stored water covered and maintained so it does not become a mosquito-breeding site. Water intended for drinking, food preparation or washing utensils must be handled and treated safely; after flooding, the Ministry of Health specifically advises treating water before those uses.
📊

Cross-check vendors before spending

Get multiple written quotations for solar, batteries, generators, tanks and internet equipment. Ask what is included, warranty length, who provides repairs, replacement costs and whether parts are readily available in Jamaica.

The lowest initial price is not always the lowest long-term cost. After-sales service matters enormously when an inverter, battery, pump, generator or internet device stops working.

What I learned about internet service

Do not assume “installed” means connected — and verify who is coming to your home.

Flow was recommended to me, and there was already a Flow modem in the house when I moved in. Yet I still could not get a working connection. Even after bills were paid, there were issues preventing the service from working, and I was told a technician was not being sent out for new accounts at that time.

I then tried Digicel. My customer-service experience was better, but the internet itself was inconsistent. After one outage, I was still waiting weeks later for a technician call-back, so I eventually moved to Starlink. Starlink has been more dependable for me overall, although I still find it slow at times. One thing I especially appreciate is being able to reach support directly through the Starlink app.

I also had an experience that taught me to verify every home service visit. I live in a gated community, so visitors must present identification that matches the name I give security. A man called saying he was coming to check my Digicel internet and gave me his name and a colleague’s name. When the two men arrived, neither their clothing nor their vehicle displayed Digicel branding.

Afterwards, when I contacted Digicel about the visit, I was told there was no record of Digicel sending a technician and no work order showing the visit. Understandably, that frightened me. Security confirmed that the men had checked out of the community. When I called the person who had originally contacted me, he explained that they were not direct Digicel employees but subcontractors working through Konnex, and that jobs were shared through a WhatsApp group for nearby technicians to pick up. I could not independently reconcile that explanation with what Digicel customer service told me.

My takeaway: even when a caller knows details about your service problem, verify the visit. Ask the provider for the technician’s name, contractor company and work-order/reference number. If you live in a gated community, give security only the verified names. If anything does not match, do not feel pressured to let someone into your home while you are trying to sort it out.
📶

Remote workers: build internet redundancy

If your income depends on being online, one connection may not be enough. Where affordable, keep a mobile-data option available even if you have fixed broadband or Starlink. Test hotspot performance from the exact room where you work.

Put the modem/router and essential work devices on a UPS or portable battery so a brief power cut does not automatically end your call or class.
🔎

Test service at the actual address

Ask neighbours what works on your street or inside your development. Coverage maps and recommendations are useful, but hills, construction materials, local infrastructure and even the exact part of a community can change the experience.

Before signing a long agreement, ask about installation time, cancellation terms, equipment fees and what happens when a technician visit is required.
09 Living well after the move

Build local knowledge, community and routines — not just paperwork.

🥬

Learn where locals actually shop

Tourist and high-demand areas can be expensive. Ask residents where farmers sell directly, which market days are best, and where they buy ground provisions, fruit, meat and household basics.

For bulk household shopping, PriceSmart can be useful — but compare unit prices rather than assuming bulk is automatically cheaper.
💬

Build your network intentionally

WhatsApp groups, neighbours, churches, professional organisations and community contacts can be extremely useful. In Jamaica, knowing who to call often matters.

Connections are valuable; discernment still matters. Verify advice involving money, documents or legal obligations.
🏡

Create the home security that helps you feel settled

Good exterior lighting, cameras, doorbell cameras, secure locks and knowing your neighbours can help you feel more comfortable without living in fear.

🌦️

Prepare for outages and severe weather

A small emergency kit with water, battery banks, flashlights, basic medicines, important numbers and a modest cash reserve is useful. If your work depends on internet, consider backup data/power options.

📱

Set up a Jamaican mobile number early

Local institutions, delivery drivers, brokers and service providers will need to reach you. A local number also makes everyday transactions simpler.

❤️

Do not be afraid to live

Jamaica is not a problem to solve. It is a country full of people working, raising families, building businesses, laughing, struggling and living. Meet people. Learn your community. Be observant without letting fear become your lifestyle.

Quick answers

Questions returning residents often ask

Does Returning Resident status mean my car is duty-free?

No. Jamaica Customs specifically states that Returning Residents do not receive a general duty exemption on motor vehicles. Returning Resident vehicle rules can provide special age/eligibility treatment, but applicable duties and taxes still have to be considered.

How long must I have owned my car?

Current Trade Board guidance for the Returning Resident motor-vehicle procedure states at least six months before the import-permit application. Always re-check before shipping because import rules can change.

Can I clear my own imported car?

Jamaica Customs states that motor vehicles must be cleared by a licensed Customs Broker.

Is healthcare really free in Jamaica?

Jamaica maintains a no-user-fee policy for qualifying public patients in public health facilities. Private patients, insured patients in specified circumstances and non-residents may be charged. Medicines, private care and some other costs may still fall outside the no-user-fee experience.

How long should I expect my shipment to take to clear?

There is no single guaranteed clearance time. From my own experience, planning for roughly one to two weeks after arrival is sensible, while staying ready to collect promptly once your broker or agent confirms release.
