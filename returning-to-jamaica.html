Start with the real experience

There is the official process. Then there is what actually happens.

Returning Resident information often tells you what form to complete. It does not always tell you what it feels like when your shipment arrives, the broker is unclear, the internet does not work, or you realise you have been losing money through everyday currency conversion.

I built this page around both: the things that are formally required and the practical decisions that can save you time, money and unnecessary stress.

Prepare early. Ask more questions than you think you need to. Trust — but verify.
01 Before you move

Get the Jamaican side of your life ready first.

A TRN, Returning Resident approval, local money and an organised document file will make almost every later step easier.

Start here

Confirm Returning Resident eligibility

Do not assume the concession applies automatically. Apply and get the approval process moving.

Identity

Get your TRN early

Your TRN comes up repeatedly in tax, vehicle, banking and other Jamaican processes.

Money

Build a JMD landing fund

Have Jamaican dollars available for local transactions, deposits, transport and the unexpected.

What documents should I gather before leaving?
Keep passport copies, proof of overseas residence, tax/employment records, proof of Jamaican address, Returning Resident correspondence, shipping records, vehicle documents, invoices and permits in one digital folder and one physical folder. Save copies offline too.
What should be on my household inventory?
Create a detailed list of shipped items, quantities and reasonable values. Separate used household effects from new items. Keep receipts for recently purchased items when possible and photograph valuable belongings before the container is sealed.
What else should I budget for besides shipping?
Broker fees, port/storage charges, transport, housing deposits, utilities, temporary transport, insurance, food and setup costs can overlap. Keep a contingency reserve rather than planning down to the last dollar.
02 Shipping your belongings

Know who has your things before they leave the UK.

The shipping company is only part of the chain. The person handling your belongings on the ground in Jamaica matters just as much.

Consolidate

Ask about one container

If sending a car and household goods, compare the full cost of shipping them together versus separately.

Verify

Know your Jamaican broker

Get the name, contact information and role of whoever will handle clearance before you pay.

In writing

Demand a cost breakdown

Know what is included, what is not, when storage starts and who is responsible at each stage.

What should I check on the bill of lading?
Confirm your name, consignee details, destination, container information and vehicle details. Keep the final bill of lading accessible.
How quickly should I collect my shipment?
Arrival does not mean immediate release. In my experience, allowing roughly one to two weeks for clearance is sensible, although actual timing varies. Once you receive the release or collection message, move quickly to reduce avoidable storage charges.
What about pets, plants and restricted items?
Check permits before packing. Pets and plants can require prior approvals. Never assume something can simply travel inside the container.
03 Importing your car

Do the permit work before the vehicle is on the water.

Vehicle mistakes are expensive. Current Returning Resident guidance uses a six-month ownership threshold, and Returning Resident status does not make the vehicle duty-free.

Key correction: the current ownership requirement is at least six months before the Returning Resident vehicle permit application — not one year.
Permit

Apply before shipping

Complete the Trade Board vehicle import permit process before making final shipping arrangements.

Documents

Keep proof of ownership

Have the title/export record, seller invoice and evidence showing when you bought the vehicle.

Clearance

Prepare TCC + broker

Motor vehicles require a TCC for clearance and must be cleared through a licensed Customs Broker.

What about the seller invoice?
Ask the dealer or seller for a properly completed invoice or bill of sale at the time of purchase. It is much easier than trying to reconstruct the paperwork months later.
Does a used UK vehicle need inspection?
Current rules include pre-shipment inspection/sanitisation requirements for used vehicles shipped from the UK. Confirm the approved inspection process before the vehicle leaves.
Should I let someone import the vehicle in their name?
Do not use an informal arrangement simply to avoid paperwork. The importer named in the documents carries legal and financial significance. If someone proposes a structure you do not understand, get it explained in writing first.
04 Arrival + money

Once you land, local knowledge starts saving you money.

Use JMD intentionally, compare normal prices, keep payment records and learn quickly where residents actually shop.

Currency

Use JMD where practical

Repeated card conversion and foreign-currency pricing can quietly eat into your money through rates and fees.

Protection

Use traceable payments

For major purchases, favour methods that leave a clear record and may offer dispute protection.

Local life

Ask where people really shop

Farmers, market days and local suppliers can be very different from prices in tourist-heavy areas.

What should I know about cash and transfers?
Keep useful cash, but not all of your money in cash. Avoid irreversible transfers to people you do not know simply because you are being pressured to act quickly. Use your common sense even when someone makes a situation feel urgent.
What about transport while I wait for my car?
If route taxis are limited where you live, a short-term rental can be more practical and sometimes more economical than repeated private taxis or ride-hailing. Ask residents what normal fares are before agreeing to regular transport arrangements.
Where should I buy food?
Ask when farmers bring produce directly to your local market. PriceSmart can be useful for bulk purchases, but compare unit prices. Tourist-heavy areas such as Ocho Rios, Montego Bay and Kingston can feel especially expensive.
05 Power + connectivity

Use the sun. Build backups. Verify every service visit.

If your work, income or peace of mind depends on electricity and internet, redundancy is not a luxury.

Sun

Use solar where it makes sense

Homeowners can price rooftop systems. Renters can use portable solar panels and power stations for essentials.

Internet

Keep a second connection

If you work online, keep mobile data or another backup available even when you have fixed broadband or Starlink.

Storm season

Plan backup power early

Battery storage, charged power banks and a properly used generator can make outages much easier to manage.

“The modem was already in the house. That did not mean I had working internet.”

My internet experience

Flow was recommended to me and there was already a modem in the house, but I still could not get a working connection. Even after bills were paid, unresolved issues prevented service and I was told a technician was not being sent for new accounts at that time.

Digicel customer service was better in my experience, but service was inconsistent. After one outage I was still waiting weeks later for a technician call-back, so I moved to Starlink. Starlink has been more dependable overall, though I still find it slow at times. I especially appreciate being able to reach support directly through the app.

What happened with the unexpected “Digicel technicians”?
A man called saying he was coming to check my internet and gave me his name and a colleague’s name for my gated-community security. When they arrived, neither their clothing nor vehicle displayed Digicel branding. Digicel later told me there was no work order or record of the visit. The caller then explained that they were subcontractors through Konnex and that nearby jobs were distributed through a WhatsApp group. I could not independently reconcile that explanation with what Digicel customer service told me.
My takeaway: ask the provider for the technician’s name, contractor company and work-order/reference number. If anything does not match, do not feel pressured to let someone into your home while you are sorting it out.
How can I keep electricity costs under control?
AC can become expensive if it runs all day. Use blackout or thermal curtains, fans where comfortable, and keep windows closed during peak heat if the outdoor air is hotter than the inside. Open windows early morning, evenings or after rain when the outside temperature is actually cooler.
What should renters consider for solar?
Portable solar panels paired with a battery power station can keep routers, laptops, phones and lights running without permanently altering the property. Calculate what you actually need to power and for how long before buying.
Can rainwater help?
Covered rainwater storage can support gardening, cleaning and other non-potable uses. Keep tanks covered and maintained so they do not become mosquito-breeding sites. Water intended for drinking or food preparation must be handled and treated safely.
06 Actually living here

Build a life — not just a successful move.

Community, healthcare, transportation, security and knowing who to call matter just as much as clearing the container.

Community

Find your people early

WhatsApp groups and local connections are useful, but choose carefully how you participate and who you trust.

Security

Create the home setup you need

Exterior lighting, cameras, secure locks and knowing your neighbours can help you feel settled without living in fear.

Mindset

Do not be scared to live

Jamaicans are living ordinary, rich, complicated lives too. Meet people. Learn your community. Be intentional.

What should I know about public healthcare?
Jamaica maintains a no-user-fee policy for qualifying public patients in public facilities. That does not mean every medicine, test or private service is automatically free. Many people still keep private insurance or an emergency medical fund.
Should I apply for NIS?
NIS is Jamaica’s contributory social-security programme. If you are working or self-employed, check whether registration applies to your situation and complete it early.
What about a Jamaican driving licence?
Do not assume one rule fits every returning resident. Requirements can depend on your existing licence, status, insurance and the transaction you are completing. Confirm current requirements with the Island Traffic Authority / Tax Administration. If you need a provisional licence, prepare the required photographs, medical certificate, identity/residence evidence and TRN.
What should I prepare for hurricane season?
Keep water, flashlights, charged battery banks, basic medicines, important phone numbers and a modest cash reserve. If your work depends on internet, test your backup data and power arrangements before a storm is approaching.

How ready are you?

Seven quick questions. No email required.

1. Have you confirmed Returning Resident eligibility?

2. Do you have your TRN and Jamaican money plan?

3. Do you know who will handle your shipment in Jamaica?

4. If importing a car, is the permit process ready?

5. Do you have extra funds for the first 4–8 weeks?

6. Do you have trustworthy local contacts?

7. If you rely on internet/power for work, do you have a backup?
